Challenges:

    Introduction

    The day that our picoCTF team leader told us that we were doing ALL of the picoCTF challenges in our category before March, I decided to lock in. Hard.

    Over the course of that week, I finished all of the easy picoCTF boxes, because honestly? The picoCTF "easy" boxes actually are easy. Save for a couple of them, I was able to crack all of these easy boxes within 5 minutes of starting, 10 tops.

    I guess HackTheBox's "easy" boxes had me under the influence that "easy" CTFs aren't really easy on any platform—picoCTF surprised me. I ran through all 17 available easy boxes pretty quickly. Because of the sheer volume of boxes that I'm going to be doing for pico, and given how little time I have left over, I'm starting to think it's just not going to be possible to do good writeups for all of them.

    So, I'm trying something new.

    Remember my writeup for TakeOver—how I said that that writeup was a live recreation of every thought I had while running through the box? That "live recreation" came from a set of super in-depth notes that I took as I was going through it. Since I can't do big fancy writeups for each pico box I do, I figure I'll just cut out the middleman entirely.

    What you'll find below is each actual set of notes that I took for every pico box I did, completely unfiltered. Enjoy!
