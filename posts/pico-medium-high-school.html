Challenges:

    Introduction

    If the last batch of mediums was a "day at the office," this one was the overtime shift that I swear I didn't ask for.

    These are the ones that started to push back. It wasn't a straight line from point A to flag anymore; it was more like following breadcrumb trails through things that didn't seem important—until they simply were. This is where medium really felt like medium. It wasn't just "find the flag" anymore—it was "find the flag while something's trying to get in your way." I think it's safe to say we're in pico High School now.






    What pico High School Taught Me

    Looking back at these medium boxes, the jump in difficulty wasn't really about the exploits being "harder" to understand; it was more about the environment getting harder to deal with.

    Remember 'middle school'? The 'simpler' times? Well, I said something pretty important:

    This little "foot-wetting", if you will, taught me that at this point in time, the enemy isn't the box. It's kinda just me.

    Wouldn't you know it—I'm still my own worst enemy when it comes to the basics.

    You'd think after 20+ boxes I'd have "check the robots.txt" tattooed on my arm or something, but Trickster got me GOOD there. That robots.txt would have saved me an embarrassing amount of time. It's not just robots.txt either—it's a recurring theme: the more "advanced" I think I'm getting, the more likely I am to ignore the simple stuff. caas (Cowsay as a Service) was fun! Spent a stupid amount of time wondering why my command injection wasn't working. I realized that it would have worked if I'd spelled cowsay as "cowsay" and not "cosway" every single time.

    Here's yet another callback to 'pico Middle School': persistence is everything.

    The Web Gauntlet and Irish-Name-Repo series were the ultimate test of that. It wasn't about one "aha!" moment; it was about trying 50 different versions of the same query until one finally slipped through the filter. That was one hell of a lesson:

    If you can stay patient longer than the dev stayed diligent, you win.

    Lastly, Burp Suite is AWESOME.

    Back in the easy days, I could get away with just using the browser console. Now though, the Interceptor is my best friend—it's the second set of eyes that catches everything I wouldn't actively be looking for. Whether it was the weird redirects in findme or testing NoSQL payloads, the "repeatable process" I was looking for in middle school is finally starting to click, and Burp is a big part of making that happen.

    We did it! That's all of the "standard" mediums that I've had time for before the competition starts. But there are a few more—there are always some outliers, aren't there?

    Those ones are a different story—they're the scary ones, the real heavy hitters. They deserve a full TakeOver-style breakdown, so stay tuned. I'll be back with those soon!

    Cheers!
