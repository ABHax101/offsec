Challenges:

    Introduction

    Hi again!

    Remember a few days ago when I said "The picoCTF 'easy' boxes actually are easy"? Well, inevitably, there were some outliers.

    Of course, this early on in my journey, I wasn't really going to have it all figured out right off the bat. I think that's where these "harder easy" pico boxes have really come in handy: these are what truly forced me to get the hang of this whole "web hacking" thing. The boxes we see below were the perfect level of challenge, in that they got me super lost, but when I turned to external resources for help, it never felt like I was just following a walkthrough—it felt like I was learning useful things that I'd genuinely need later on. Going into the medium boxes, I think these are the best prep I could have had, because through sheer experience, they taught me how to navigate technical hurdles, but they also taught me how NOT dump hours of my life into the wrong rabbit hole.

    Essentially, this was like picoCTF middle school. And once again, what you'll find below is (almost) every single thought I had along the way.






    What pico Middle School Taught Me

    These last few easy boxes were more than just three more flags.

    Essentially, this little "foot-wetting", if you will, taught me that at this point in time, the enemy isn't the box. It's kinda just me.

    First off, there was the fact that I was barely doing any recon. The fact that the other pico "easy" boxes had been so easy was getting to my head, and I kinda started relying entirely on the names of the boxes and the little blurb to carry me through them.

    It seems pretty obvious right now, but that can't work—you can't hack what you can't see. The fact that a simple gobuster dir -w common.txt -u "<url here>" would have saved me literal hours on Crack the Gate 1 was... alarming.

    Further on that end, it was made clear to me that persistence is key in pretty much ALL of this. In SSTI1, by the time I'd exhausted all my new TryHackMe learning, I was fairly discouraged. Going from a bunch of "Inspect Element" to an actual technical exploit was a big jump, but then I learned that it was simply a task of research, not a wall.

    Speaking of persistence, Cookies really tested me. The box really was easy—it was just tedious as hell. It was a nice callback to Week 3's recurring lesson:

    Until proven innocent, EVERYTHING is guilty.


    Ultimately, these boxes were the wake-up call I needed. They turned 'web hacking' from a bunch of lucky guesses into a repeatable process. I'm not a pro yet, but I’m done relying on luck.
