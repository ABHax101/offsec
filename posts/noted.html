    Introduction

    Hello again!

    Before we get into this one, there's a problem that I need to address. Back in December of last year, when I wrote my very first post in this blog, the plan was to finish TryHackMe's Jr. Penetration Tester pathway in the first 2-3 months of the new year, and then spend the rest of the time I had before summer break on TryHackMe, HackTheBox, picoCTF, etc. in order to do as much practical work with those skills as I could. The plan for summer was to spend that time doing my capstone project.

    Here's the problem. We're halfway through June already.

    It's been so long since I've had time to log into TryHackMe that they've literally revamped the entire curriculum that I was following: my percent completion of the pathway went from about 50% to now only 12%. I am RIDICULOUSLY behind schedule right now.

    So I need time to get back into TryHackMe and make generational progress, while also keeping this blog up-to-date, making time for college tours and essay writing, as well as family trips and other summer fun, and somehow ALSO leaving enough time for me to do my capstone project this summer—real security audits of real apps, done as close to professionally as I can.

    That's a lot of stuff to keep track of at once, and that time has to come from somewhere. So here's the plan:

    First: I'm not going to publishing any more writeups.

    That sounds crazy—am I just ending the blog here?

    No. Absolutely not.

    The blog isn't ending here, and you'll still get the full story, beginning to end, of everything I do—it's just that it'll be in the form of my raw, unprocessed notes from each box, TryHackMe module, etc. I'm cutting the middleman: all that my formal 'writeups' do is retell those same notes, but in prose. That's really not adding all that much; the notes themselves are already a story. And for any context we need in order to understand, I'm here. That's a 10-minute job. Way better than multiple hours.

    Second: the capstone project needs an update.

    The idea I outlined in December of last year for the capstone project was to find 3-4 open-source apps, contact the developers for permission, do full security audits of each of them, and post them all on my blog.

    You know how hard that would be?

    Finding a maintainer, getting permission, scoping what I'm allowed to test, waiting for responses—that's a project in itself. And forget 3-4 apps, that could take an absurd amount of time even for one. In theory, this idea of reaching out and testing others' apps is absolutely doable. In practice, school will probably start before I even get past all the paperwork—I don't got time like that anymore.

    So here's the idea: instead of digging around GitHub, asking developers if I break their code for my portfolio, I'm going to audit an app that I know really well. The chat app. The one I made in 7th grade (see my first post). The one that I built with absolutely zero security knowledge. It's a gold mine of vulnerabilities; now I have the skills to systematically take it apart and explain exactly how and why.

    And that's a complete arc, with a beginning, middle, and end. That chat app was essentially the thing that started it all; auditing it as a kind of exodus only feels right. It closes the loop.

    Oh, and also...I made that app. I'm the developer. I hereby give myself permission to break absolutely everything, and then post it online. There—that wasn't so hard.


    So, there's the plan. Hop back on TryHackMe and finish all that I can, cut the middleman for the blog, and end it off on a full-circle moment.
